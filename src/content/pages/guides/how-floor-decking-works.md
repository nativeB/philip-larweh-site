## What floor decking is

When you pour a concrete floor or roof slab, the concrete needs something to sit on until it sets. **Floor decking** is that temporary platform. It's built, the steel goes on top, the concrete is poured, and once the slab is strong enough the decking comes down and the materials are used again on the next job. That's why most builders **hire** decking materials instead of buying them.

## The materials

- **Props** hold everything up. Bamboo props are common on Ghanaian sites. → [Bamboo props for hire](/materials/bamboo-props/)
- **Timber joists**, usually **wawa 2/4**, run across the props and carry the deck. → [Wawa 2/4 for hire](/materials/wawa/)
- **Plywood boards** form the flat surface the concrete is poured onto. Film-faced plywood gives a smoother finish. → [Plywood for hire](/materials/plywood/)
- **Wawa boards** and plywood are also used for the beam sides and edges. → [Wawa boards](/materials/wawa/)
- **Column moulds** shape round columns. → [Round column moulds](/materials/column-moulds/)

## The steps

1. **Prop it up**: props are set out under the slab and beams.
2. **Build the beam formwork**: beam boxes are built along the beam lines.
3. **Lay the deck**: joists go across, then plywood boards on top, level and tight.
4. **Line and check**: many sites line the deck with polythene sheeting before the steel goes on.
5. **Steel bending**: slab mesh and beam cages are tied in place on the deck. → [Steel bending](/services/steel-bending/)
6. **Pour and cure**: the concrete is poured and left to gain strength.
7. **Strike**: once your engineer says the slab is ready, the decking and props come down.
